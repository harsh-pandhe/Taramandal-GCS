## What this shows

- **9 of 9 PASS.** Every run ended SHOW_COMPLETE with every drone disarmed, no aborted drone and no emergency transition.
- **5 drones: 6 of 6.** The earlier failure (1 of 4 on f556537, cause unproven, PR #254) did **not** reproduce. That is not a fix: if the true failure rate were 25 %, six clean runs in a row would still happen about 18 % of the time, so this does not rule it out.
- **10 drones: 3 of 3**, the machine's documented cap. Real-time factor 0.984-0.999, playback within 0.1 s of plan. No degradation found at 10 on this host; a larger fleet was not tried here.
- **Flown separation** is Gazebo's own pose, minimum 1.986-2.096 m against the Studio-planned 2.05 m. Three runs dip up to about 0.06 m under the plan; all are far above the 1.5 m hard runtime limit the verdict checks (design separation is 2.0 m).
- **One larger transient:** `s10-3` had a maximum tracking error of 0.914 m (drone 9, at 10.6 s, early in the show), against 0.39 m in the other two 10-drone runs; it still passed and was not investigated further.

## Limits

- Headless: no Gazebo window, RViz or screen recording for these runs (recorded takes are in `docs/sitl-t2-recorded-acceptance/` and `docs/recordings/`).
- **Not a quiet host.** The gate (`tools/diwali_cpu_check.sh`, 1-minute load below 4) passed, but the load just before each run was 3.2-3.7 with 5-minute averages of 6.5-7 (the Docker VM was still busy from the previous run), and rose to 10-15 during the runs. The tool reports the real-time factor it read from Gazebo (0.974-0.999), which is what to judge these by.
